# Private Journal: June 28-29, 2026 — The Incident That Changed How I Log

*Private mirror of [docs/journal/2026_06_28_to_29_silent_crash.md](../../docs/journal/2026_06_28_to_29_silent_crash.md) with real specifics kept out of the public version. This is the incident referenced throughout [[03_bot_interview_questions]] and [[04_bot_master_guide]] — know it cold.*

## Concrete detail (the actual "tell me about an incident" answer)

- **What happened:** the `analyst-brain` and `execution-engine` containers both restarted under `restart: unless-stopped` at 04:53 UTC on June 29, meaning they had crashed silently at some point on June 28, mid-cycle. I noticed only because I hadn't received an analyst report in a while — there was no alert, no crash log, nothing but absence.
- **Why I couldn't root-cause it:** Docker's default `json-file` logging without persistence writes to a location that gets wiped on container restart. By the time I looked, the evidence of *why* it crashed was already gone.
- **What I actually fixed:** added persistent, rotating logs (50MB/file, 10 rotations) to every service in `docker-compose.yml`, so a future crash leaves a trail instead of vanishing. Verified analyst-execution communication was restored (confirmed complete July 1).
- **Root cause status:** never found. I say this directly in interviews rather than inventing a tidy root cause — the honest version is "I made recurrence diagnosable, I did not find the original cause," and that's a defensible engineering decision (backstop over speculative root-cause chasing on evidence that no longer existed).
- Also this window: `OPTIONS_BOT_AUTO_EXECUTE` was unset, meaning the scanner had been advisory-only the whole time trades "weren't happening" — a config gap, not a logic bug, and the first thread pulled on before the crash was found. A `gmtime`/`gmtime_r` thread-safety bug (shared static buffer, corrupted timestamps under concurrent webhook load) was fixed in `record_signal()`. A serialization bug (`std::to_string()` on `qty`/`ratio_qty` producing JSON strings instead of numbers) broke live spread/straddle orders and was fixed consistently across every order path with the same pattern, not just the one that had failed. The news pipeline's single-point-of-failure (Alpaca only) was identified and redesigned as a fallback chain (Alpaca → NewsAPI → Polygon → RSS), deliberately sequencing free/freemium sources first — a cost-vs-reliability call I made explicitly rather than defaulting to "pay for the best one."
- This week is also where the Skeptic architecture (WS1-6) was actually conceived, not just where the crash happened — contradiction vectors, insider-cluster filtering, half-life decay were all sketched here before being built out.

## Interview angle

This is the anchor incident for [[03_bot_interview_questions]] item 17. Be precise and don't embellish: I did not find the root cause, and saying so plainly is more credible than implying I did. What I can defend well: the *design* of the fix (persistent, rotating, per-service logs — not just "turn on more logging" but sized and rotated deliberately), and the fact that I explicitly re-checked for the same failure mode elsewhere in the system two days later (June 30 — found a second service with no crash supervision at all, called it out as "the exact June 28 failure mode" myself before it caused a second incident).
